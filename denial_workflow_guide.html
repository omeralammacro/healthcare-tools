Section 04

Status Guide — What Each Status Means & When to Use It

Important: Always select the status that most accurately reflects the action you took or the current state of the claim. Using the wrong status delays resolution tracking and skews reporting.
Status What It Means When to Use It
Reprocessing
Insurance Reprocessing		 Insurance agreed to reprocess the claim under the original submission without changes. Use when you called or used the portal and the insurance representative confirmed they will reprocess the claim. A reference number should always be obtained and noted.
Coding
Coding Corrected		 A coding error (CPT, ICD, modifier, etc.) was identified and corrected on the claim. Use after the billing/coding team has corrected the code and the corrected claim has been submitted. Note the original code and the corrected code in your AR note.
Claim
Corrected Claim Mailed		 A corrected version of the claim has been physically mailed or faxed to the payer. Use when the corrected claim was sent by mail or fax — not electronically. Always note the date mailed, fax confirmation number, or tracking number.
Appeal
Appeal Claim		 A formal written appeal has been submitted to the insurance company disputing the denial. Use after the appeal letter and supporting documentation have been submitted. Note the appeal reference number, date submitted, and expected turnaround. Follow up within 30 days.
Claim
Appealed / Med Recs		 Appeal has been submitted and medical records were included as supporting documentation. Use when medical records were specifically attached to the appeal package. Document which records were included and the dates of the records.
Filing
Filed		 The claim has been filed (or re-filed) with the payer. Use when a claim is being submitted or resubmitted electronically after a denial or rejection. Note the submission date and confirmation number if available.
Pending
Follow Up		 Action has been taken and the claim is awaiting a response. A follow-up is scheduled. Use as a general pending status when you've taken action but are waiting for insurance to respond. Always pair with a follow-up date of 10–15 days.
Auth
No Auth-Referral Denial		 The claim was denied because prior authorization or a referral was not obtained or on file. Use when the denial EOB specifically cites missing authorization or referral. Note steps taken to obtain retro auth, referral from PCP, or next action planned.
Pending
Pending B&E		 Claim is pending Benefits & Eligibility verification. Use when eligibility needs to be confirmed before the claim can be resolved. Contact eligibility department or check the portal and document findings.
Pending
COB		 Coordination of Benefits — another insurance may be primary. Use when the patient has multiple insurances and COB must be established or confirmed. Note which payer is primary, which is secondary, and steps taken to coordinate.
Pending
Pending Coding		 The claim is on hold waiting for a coding review or correction from the coding team. Use when you've identified a coding issue and have sent it to the coding team for review. Do not finalize or submit until coding has responded.
Pending
Pending Payment		 The claim has been approved and payment is in transit or being processed by the payer. Use when insurance has confirmed approval and payment is expected within a stated timeframe. Note the expected payment date and amount provided by the rep.
Pending
Pending Write-off		 The account is awaiting write-off approval per practice policy. Use when the balance meets write-off criteria (contractual, bad debt, etc.) and is pending supervisor or billing manager approval. Do not write off without authorization.
Filing
Timely Filing		 The claim was denied because it was submitted past the payer's timely filing deadline. Use when the denial reason is timely filing. Gather proof of timely filing (original submission reports, clearinghouse records) and submit an appeal with documentation.
EOB
Secondary Needs Primary EOB		 The secondary payer requires the primary payer's Explanation of Benefits (EOB) to process the claim. Use when you have submitted to secondary but they require the primary EOB. Obtain the primary EOB and resubmit. Note the date EOB was requested or obtained.
Review
Practice Review		 The claim has been flagged for internal practice review before further action is taken. Use when the claim requires provider review, compliance check, or clinical documentation review before the next step. Document who it was sent to and the expected response date.
Rejected
Rejected		 The claim was rejected by the payer before processing due to a technical or data error. Use when the claim did not pass payer edits — wrong member ID, missing NPI, invalid dates, etc. Correct the error and resubmit. Note the rejection code and corrective action.
Resolved
Paid		 The claim has been paid in full or partially by the insurance company. Use when payment has been confirmed and posted. If partially paid, document the paid amount, balance, and whether a secondary claim or patient statement is needed.
Resolved
Status Updated to Paid		 Status is being manually updated to reflect payment already received and confirmed. Use when the system status needs to be updated to reflect a payment that has been posted. Always verify in the payment ledger before selecting this status.
Client
Pending Client Response		 Awaiting a response from the client (provider, practice, or facility) before action can proceed. Use when you need clinical information, a signed document, or a decision from the provider before you can move forward. Document what was requested and the date requested.
Section 05

How to Write a Professional AR Note

Every action on every account must have a complete, professional AR note. Notes are a legal and operational record. Vague, incomplete, or missing notes create re-work, delay resolution, and are flagged in audits.

Required Information — Every Note

  • DateDate the action was taken (MM/DD/YYYY)
  • AgentYour name or agent ID
  • DOSDate of service the claim is for
  • CPTProcedure code(s) on the claim being worked
  • PayerName of the insurance company contacted
  • ActionExact action taken — called / portal / patient contact
  • Rep NameName of the insurance representative spoken to
  • Ref #Reference or call confirmation number provided
  • Denial ReasonThe reason for denial as stated on the EOB
  • OutcomeResult of the call or action — what was resolved or confirmed
  • Next StepClearly state what happens next and who is responsible
  • F/U DateFollow-up date set (10–15 days out)

Situational Fields — Include When Applicable

  • Appeal RefAppeal reference number when an appeal is submitted
  • Auth #Authorization number if auth was obtained or retro-approved
  • ICD-10Diagnosis code if relevant to the denial reason
  • ModifierInclude modifier if that was the root cause of denial
  • NPIProvider NPI if there was an NPI mismatch or credentialing issue
  • TC/26Technical component / professional component if billing split services
  • Claim #Insurance's internal claim number (different from your claim number)
  • EOB DateDate of the EOB being referenced in the note
  • Fax Conf #Fax confirmation number when documents were faxed
  • Paid AmtIf partial payment was received, note the amount paid and balance
Sample AR Note — Call to Insurance
04/01/2025 | Agent: J. Rivera | DOS: 01/10/2025 | CPT: 99214 | Payer: Blue Cross Blue Shield PPO Action: Called BCBS at 1-800-123-4567. Spoke with Rep: Sarah M. | Ref #: BCB-20250401-9921. Denial reason per EOB dated 01/18/2025: Prior authorization not on file. Rep confirmed claim is eligible for retro authorization. Retro auth submitted via portal — Auth #: RA-20250401-114. Rep confirmed claim will be reprocessed within 10–14 business days. Next step: Monitor for payment. Follow-up date set: 04/15/2025. Billing task added. Status updated to: Insurance Reprocessing.

✓ What to Do

  • Always include the date of service (DOS) and CPT code in every note
  • Get the rep's name and a reference number on every call
  • State the exact denial reason from the EOB — do not paraphrase vaguely
  • Describe exactly what you did: called, used portal, faxed, etc.
  • State the outcome clearly — what was resolved, approved, or confirmed
  • Include TC/26 when billing technical or professional components separately
  • Specify the follow-up date in the note body, not just the task
  • Include modifier if that was the source of the denial
  • Note the auth number when authorization was the denial reason
  • Keep notes factual, chronological, and in the third person
  • Update the status in the system before saving the note
  • Include the EOB date when referencing a specific remittance

✗ What Not to Do

  • Do not write vague notes like "called insurance" with no outcome
  • Do not skip the rep name or reference number — always ask for both
  • Do not copy-paste the same note repeatedly without updating the content
  • Do not leave a note without a clear next step or follow-up date
  • Do not use abbreviations the next agent won't understand
  • Do not write notes in first person ("I called…") — use third person ("Agent called…")
  • Do not omit the CPT code — it's essential for context on multi-code claims
  • Do not select the wrong status before saving — it cannot be easily corrected
  • Do not record patient-sensitive info (SSN, full DOB) in open note fields
  • Do not close a note without confirming whether payment or resolution occurred
  • Do not assume — if the rep gave conflicting info, document both versions
  • Do not skip the billing task — no task means no Bucket 2 follow-up

Additional AR Note Standards

  • Portal Notes When using the insurance portal instead of calling, note the portal name, the action taken (status check, claim submission, auth request), and the result shown on screen. Screenshot or confirmation number should be referenced if available.
  • Patient Contact When contacting the patient, document: date called, whether you spoke to the patient or left a voicemail, the reason for the call, any information provided by the patient, and the next step. Never document clinical information shared by the patient in the AR note field — route to the appropriate clinical documentation area.
  • Appeal Notes When submitting an appeal, note: date submitted, method (fax / mail / portal), appeal reference number, documents included (clinical notes, auth records, medical records), and expected turnaround time stated by the payer.
  • Corrected Claims When submitting a corrected claim, note: original claim number, correction made (code, modifier, date, NPI, etc.), date resubmitted, and submission confirmation number. Always note who authorized the correction if coding was involved.
  • No Contact / No Answer If you attempted a call and there was no answer or the line was unavailable, document the attempt: time called, number called, result (no answer / voicemail / busy), and the next planned action. Three documented contact attempts are typically required before escalating.
