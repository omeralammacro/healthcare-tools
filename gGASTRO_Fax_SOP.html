05 — Exceptions & Special Scenarios
⚠️ Exceptions require additional steps before filing or tasking. Never rush past an exception — incorrect handling can result in documents going to the wrong chart, lost records, or missed clinical actions.
👥
Single Fax Contains Multiple Patients

When one fax document contains information for more than one patient, you must split it before filing.

  1. Identify every patient name/MRN appearing in the document.
  2. Split the document into individual files — one per patient.
  3. Rename each file correctly for its respective fax type.
  4. File each document into the correct patient's chart in gGASTRO.
  5. Task each document independently per the SOP for that fax type.

⚠️ Never file a multi-patient document into a single chart.

❓
Patient Not Found in gGASTRO EHR

If you cannot locate the patient in gGASTRO after a thorough search, this may be a misdirected fax.

  1. Search by full name, date of birth, and phone number — not just name alone.
  2. Try alternate spellings or nicknames (e.g., Bob vs. Robert).
  3. If still not found, check the fax header for the intended recipient's fax number — it may be a wrong-number fax.
  4. Flag as a possible mistake fax and do NOT file it in any chart.
  5. Notify your supervisor before discarding — the document may still need to be forwarded to the correct provider.
  6. Document the incident with: fax date, sender info, patient name on fax, action taken.
📋
Medical Records Request — Multiple Patients

When a single fax is a records request covering multiple patients (not records received):

  1. Rename: Request for medical records for multiple patients
  2. File under: Request for Records
  3. Task the entire list directly to Irma without pre-checking charts.
  4. In the task note: "Sending full list — please check each patient and send me and Shiv a task from their chart if records are missing."

MRN ref: 8066

📅
Consult Note — Patient Has No Upcoming Appointment

If the patient was last seen by Dr. George Tan and has no upcoming appointment scheduled:

  1. Do not task to Dr. George Tan's queue.
  2. Task the consult note to Dr. James Han instead.
  3. Include note: "Patient last seen by Dr. George Tan on [date]. No upcoming appointment scheduled as of now."
👩‍⚕️

The following providers do NOT follow the standard "last seen provider" routing for received medical records:

  • Maria Roy

🚫
Pulmonology Clearance — Procedure Cancelled

If a Pulmonology Clearance arrives but the procedure it relates to has been cancelled:

  1. Verify that the procedure is in fact cancelled in gGASTRO.
  2. Do NOT task to Daniela, Brenda, Kimberly, the procedure provider, Priyanka, or Nidhi.
  3. Task the document only to Danny and Kathy.
  4. Include a note explaining the procedure was cancelled.
🧬
Cologuard — Result-Dependent Routing

Routing for Cologuard results depends entirely on whether the result is positive or negative:

  1. Read the result clearly — confirm Positive or Negative before any action.
  2. Negative: Task to Nidhi AND Mahima.
  3. Positive: Task to the ordering provider only.
  4. Do not task a positive result to Nidhi/Mahima — they are for negatives only.

Instruction from Dr. James Han, 06/23/2025

🏠
BrightStar Home Health — Action Required

BrightStar faxes contain care plans that must be reviewed, signed, and returned.

  1. Rename: Home Health Certification and Plan of Care - BrightStar Care
  2. File under: General Correspondence
  3. Task to Neyda with note: "Please review, sign, and return to BrightStar Care as soon as possible."

06 — Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them
🚨 These are the most frequent errors made when processing faxes. Read and review regularly — especially if you are new to the role.
01
Tasking Auth Approval to the Wrong Person
Authorization approvals look identical regardless of type. Without identifying whether the auth is for a procedure, office visit, or imaging, the fax gets tasked incorrectly — delaying care or billing.
Always read the auth document to determine what it covers (procedure = Danny/Kathy, office visit = Mahima, imaging = Diana Barrios) before tasking.
HIGH RISK
02
Filing a Multi-Patient Fax Into One Chart
When a fax contains more than one patient's information, filing the entire document into the first patient's chart contaminates records — another patient's PHI is now visible where it shouldn't be.
Always scan the full fax before filing. If multiple patients are present, split the document before entering any chart.
HIGH RISK
03
Skipping the SNOMED CT Code on Consult Notes
Consult notes require orders to be checked and closed, and a SNOMED CT code to be added. Forgetting this step leaves open orders in the system and creates billing/coding inaccuracies.
Before tasking any Consult Note, always check for open orders and enter the SNOMED CT code. Make it a checklist item.
HIGH RISK
04
Not Searching the Patient Thoroughly Before Labeling a "Mistake Fax"
Declaring a fax misdirected without doing a full search (by DOB, phone, alternate name spelling) can result in legitimate patient documents being discarded and clinical actions missed.
Always search by name + DOB + phone. Try alternate spellings. Only escalate as "not found" after exhausting all search options.
HIGH RISK
05
Reversing Findings and Reason in EGD / Colo W/ Pathology
EGD and Colonoscopy with pathology require Findings = Postoperative and Reason = Preoperative. Reversing these causes incorrect Dx Study documentation and can affect clinical decision-making.
Memorize: Findings → Postoperative | Reason → Preoperative. Double-check before saving every EGD/Colo Dx Study entry.
HIGH RISK
06
Forgetting to Message Mahima on Teams for Consult Notes & Follow-ups
Tasking the provider for signature is only half the job for Consult Notes and Follow-up Notes. The Teams message to Mahima ("MRN# Referral loop closed") is a separate, required step that is easy to forget.
Create a habit: after tasking the provider, immediately open Teams and send Mahima the MRN confirmation message before moving to the next fax.
MEDIUM
07
Tasking a Positive Cologuard Result to Nidhi & Mahima
Positive Cologuard results must go to the ordering provider, not Nidhi and Mahima. Sending a positive result there means the clinical team may not see it promptly, delaying follow-up care.
Before tasking Cologuard: read the result. Negative = Nidhi + Mahima. Positive = ordering provider only.
HIGH RISK
08
Not Closing Orders for Imaging or Hospital Records
Imaging results and hospital records require open related orders to be closed in gGASTRO. Forgetting this leaves "pending" orders cluttering the system, causing confusion for providers and billing teams.
After filing Imaging or Hospital Records, always navigate to the Orders section and verify there are no open orders to close before tasking the provider.
MEDIUM
09
Using Incomplete or Vague File Names
Renaming a fax as just "Medical Records" or "Imaging" without specifying the facility or patient name makes documents extremely hard to locate later, especially in audits or when providers request specific records.
Always follow the full naming convention. Include facility names, provider names, and patient names exactly as specified in the SOP for each fax type.
MEDIUM
10
Filing Imaging as "Outside Office Visits" Instead of "Imaging Results"
Imaging documents are sometimes mistakenly filed under Outside Office Visits, especially if they arrive alongside other clinical notes. This breaks the EHR filing structure and makes imaging invisible in the correct location.
If the document contains an imaging study or radiology report — even if it came with other records — it belongs under "Imaging Results," not "Outside Office Visits."
MEDIUM
11
Routing Medical Records Exception Providers Without Checking With Nidhi
Maria Roy, Maria Jumalon, Erick Cartaya Aguilar, and George Tan have non-standard routing for received medical records. Auto-assigning to "last seen provider" for these four providers sends the document to the wrong place.
Memorize these four names. When you see them on a Medical Records Received fax, stop and ask Nidhi before tasking.
MEDIUM
12
Sending Pulmonology Clearance to Full Team When Procedure is Cancelled
Sending a clearance for a cancelled procedure to the full clearance team (Daniela, Brenda, Kimberly, etc.) causes unnecessary confusion and workload for staff whose patient no longer has an active procedure.
Always verify in gGASTRO whether the procedure is still active before routing a Pulmonology Clearance. If cancelled → Danny and Kathy only.
LOW
13
Forgetting the Dx Study Entry for Imaging Results
Imaging faxes require a Dx Study to be completed in gGASTRO. Skipping this means the study is not formally linked to the patient's diagnostic record, breaking the clinical documentation trail.
Treat "Dx Study = Yes" as a mandatory checkbox. After filing Imaging Results, complete the Dx Study section before moving on.
MEDIUM
14
Routing Ambulatory PH Monitor Reports to Anyone Other Than Dr. James Han
This report type has a standing instruction — it always goes to Dr. James Han, regardless of who ordered it or who the patient last saw. Any deviation breaks this standing clinical directive.
Ambulatory PH Monitor Report = Dr. James Han, every single time. No exceptions. This was a direct standing instruction from Dr. Han.
HIGH RISK
15
Pre-Checking Records Before Passing Multi-Patient Records Request to Irma
For bulk records requests covering multiple patients, some staff attempt to verify each patient's records themselves before forwarding to Irma. This is incorrect — the SOP specifies to send the full list to Irma without pre-checking.
Send the complete list to Irma with the standard task note. Let Irma and Shiv handle the individual record checks from each patient's chart.
LOW
